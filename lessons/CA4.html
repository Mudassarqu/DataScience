Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Unit objective: Visualise liquid temperature patterns to monitor heat fluctuations, support smart climate control and promote energy-efficient responses.

Problem context (community need): Warm seawater harms corals and fish farms along the UAE coast, and pool and storage-tank temperatures swing with the weather. A low-cost floating or handheld monitor lets communities see warming early.

Design task: Design a physical, 3D-printable / laser-cut housing that holds the Arduino and integrates the Waterproof temperature probe, so a community can read and use the data.

User: a marine club, a fish farm or a school science lab.

Data Science Link

Your product exists to produce good data. This is how the unit's Four Big Ideas connect to the design.

What data does the sensor give? Measures temperature digitally in °C, ranging from –55 °C to +125 °C.

Low end: Lower values: cooler water or liquid, so stable or night-time conditions.
High end: Higher values: warmer water, so exposure to heat, sunlight or global warming.

How the data is useful: Systems can regulate heating or cooling based on liquid temperature, conserving energy and water.

1 · CollectionThe sensor reads the world; the housing must not disturb it.
2 · OrganisationArduino sends readings to Python; clean errors, missing values and duplicates.
3 · AnalysisLine, bar and pie charts reveal patterns and compare places or times.
4 · InterpretationThe community decides what to do with the pattern.

Modelling Worksheet — Making the Physical Model

Follow in order. Lab tools: 3D printer (PETG), laser cutter, hex driver, 320-grit sandpaper, hot glue gun.

  1. Check the CAD: open the chosen STL in TinkerCAD or your slicer, set units to millimetres and confirm the envelope is 156.0 × 156.0 × 224.0 mm (limit 170 × 170 × 240 mm).
  2. Slice the printed parts (Beacon, Dry box, Float ring, Float spoke, Mast) in Cura/PrusaSlicer: PETG, 0.2 mm layers, 15–20% infill, supports only where an overhang needs them.
  3. Laser-cut the MDF parts (Mounting hub) from 3 mm MDF using the top orthographic view as the footprint.
  4. De-burr and sand every printed part (320 grit); check the sensor opening and screw holes so nothing binds or blocks the sensor.
  5. Fit the sensor: Feed the probe cable through a sealed gland so the steel probe hangs at least 150 mm below the float, and silicone around the gland.
  6. Assemble following the numbered exploded view and the BOM: fasteners finger-tight first.
  7. Wire and test: connect the sensor to the Arduino, upload your test sketch (Criterion C), and watch the readings in the Serial Monitor.
  8. Install in the community location from your design brief and confirm the sensor sees what it should.
  9. Function-test against specifications 7.1 and 7.2: Measures liquid temperature to within ±1 °C of a reference thermometer. Record the results in your portfolio.
  10. Finish: spray or vinyl-wrap in the colour scheme from specification 1.1, then re-test.
