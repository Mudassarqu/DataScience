Design Brief

Context that frames the whole Criterion B response for this lesson.

Unit objective: Detect ground-level movements or shocks that may indicate construction activity, nearby traffic or early signs of earthquakes.

Problem context (community need): Heavy machinery and traffic can weaken old structures, and minor tremors can go unnoticed. A cheap shake sensor on a wall, bridge or ground spike gives early warning.

Design task: Design a physical, 3D-printable / laser-cut housing that holds the Arduino and integrates the Vibration sensor, so a community can read and use the data.

User: engineers, schools and city planners.

Data Science Link

Your product exists to produce good data. This is how the unit's Four Big Ideas connect to the design.

What data does the sensor give? Measures vibration levels in structures and surfaces using analog values.

Low end: Lower values: a stable surface with no significant vibration.
High end: Higher values: increased vibration from machinery, construction or minor tremors.

How the data is useful: Early detection of structural stress helps authorities keep schools, offices and busy areas safe.

1 · CollectionThe sensor reads the world; the housing must not disturb it.
2 · OrganisationArduino sends readings to Python; clean errors, missing values and duplicates.
3 · AnalysisLine, bar and pie charts reveal patterns and compare places or times.
4 · InterpretationThe community decides what to do with the pattern.

Modelling Worksheet — Making the Physical Model

Follow in order. Lab tools: 3D printer (PETG), laser cutter, hex driver, 320-grit sandpaper, hot glue gun.

  1. Check the CAD: open the chosen STL in TinkerCAD or your slicer, set units to millimetres and confirm the envelope is 152.6 × 134.0 × 157.0 mm (limit 170 × 150 × 170 mm).
  2. Slice the printed parts (Housing, Housing cap, Spike foot, Tripod leg) in Cura/PrusaSlicer: PETG, 0.2 mm layers, 15–20% infill, supports only where an overhang needs them.
  3. Laser-cut the MDF parts (Tripod hub plate) from 3 mm MDF using the top orthographic view as the footprint.
  4. De-burr and sand every printed part (320 grit); check the sensor opening and screw holes so nothing binds or blocks the sensor.
  5. Fit the sensor: Fix the vibration switch rigidly to the housing cap with two bolts, and tighten the feet after setting the unit level.
  6. Assemble following the numbered exploded view and the BOM: fasteners finger-tight first.
  7. Wire and test: connect the sensor to the Arduino, upload your test sketch (Criterion C), and watch the readings in the Serial Monitor.
  8. Install in the community location from your design brief and confirm the sensor sees what it should.
  9. Function-test against specifications 7.1 and 7.2: Detects a tap on the structure and reports a spike in under 1 second, with at least 10 clearly distinguishable levels. Record the results in your portfolio.
  10. Finish: spray or vinyl-wrap in the colour scheme from specification 1.1, then re-test.
